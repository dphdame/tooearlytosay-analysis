Private Medicaid managed care plans now cover three-quarters of enrollees nationwide. Because plans receive fixed monthly capitation payments, a standard policy view holds that private plans screen out provider fraud and boost state enforcement output.

When we pair 15 years of state Medicaid Fraud Control Unit records with federal managed care series across 51 jurisdictions (755 state-years, FY 2010 to 2024), within-state managed care expansions show no detectable association with convictions per staff-year (slope = -0.007, p = 0.981, 95% CI [-7.7%, +7.5%] per +10 pp).

Yet convictions per staff-year vary 17.9-fold across states. In a descriptive three-stage log-variance accounting identity, Stage 2 triage (indictments per opened case) accounts for 75.4% of cross-state variance overall and 84.2% for fraud-only cases.

In FY 2024, state units opened cases on only 23% of plan referrals, pointing to a testable hypothesis around referral rules and triage capacity.
