When a panel regression on a final outcome returns no detectable association, the aggregate slope alone cannot tell us where the production funnel varies.

When we write the outcome as a product of stage ratios and take natural logs, cross-unit variance becomes an exact covariance split that sums to 100%.

Applying that diagnostic to 15 years of state Medicaid fraud records separates two findings:

1. Within states, expanding managed care enrollment shows no detectable association with convictions per staff-year at state-year precision.

2. Across states, a descriptive three-stage log-variance accounting identity shows that case-to-indictment triage accounts for 75.4% of log conviction variance, while state units open cases on 23% of plan referrals.

Stopping at the aggregate panel slope misses where dispersion sits; decomposing the funnel locates the stage where referral rules and triage capacity can be tested.
