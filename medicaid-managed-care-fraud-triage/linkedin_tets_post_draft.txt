What a public enforcement rate measures depends on how upstream contractors and state prosecutors divide the pipeline. Because capitated Medicaid plans receive fixed monthly payments and bear claims risk, a standard policy view holds that private plans screen out provider billing fraud and raise state conviction output.

When we pair state Medicaid Fraud Control Unit records with federal managed care series across all U.S. states, two patterns stand out:

1. Within states, expanding managed care enrollment shows no detectable association with convictions per staff-year at state-year precision.

2. Across states, a descriptive three-stage log-variance accounting identity shows that investigative triage (indictments per opened case) accounts for 75.4% of cross-state conviction variance, while state units open cases on only 23% of plan referrals.

That pattern points to a testable hypothesis around referral documentation floors and forensic triage capacity.
